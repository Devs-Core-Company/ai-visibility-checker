Your child’s first dental visit

By Tom Ellis, Dental therapist · Published 12 November 2024 · Updated 1 December 2024

Key points from this article

  • When to come: Bring your child when their first tooth appears, or by their first birthday at the latest.
  • What happens: Most first visits take ten minutes.
  • Preparing your child: Read a picture book about visiting the dentist, play at counting teeth at home and avoid words such as hurt or injection.
  • After the visit: Praise your child and plan the next visit for six months later.

When to come

Bring your child when their first tooth appears, or by their first birthday at the latest. Early visits are about getting used to the dentist, not treatment.

What happens

Most first visits take ten minutes. Your child can sit on your lap while the dentist counts their teeth and checks the gums. We give advice on brushing, teething and drinks.

Preparing your child

Read a picture book about visiting the dentist, play at counting teeth at home and avoid words such as hurt or injection. Book a time when your child is usually rested.

After the visit

Praise your child and plan the next visit for six months later. Regular visits build trust before any treatment is ever needed.

If you have questions about anything in this article, our team can help at your next appointment. Read about general dentistry at Harbourline or book a visit online.
