Do my wisdom teeth need to come out?

By Dr Rohan Mehta, Implant dentist · Published 20 April 2026 · Updated 1 May 2026

Key points from this article

  • Usually not: Most wisdom teeth that come through without trouble, or that stay buried without symptoms, can be left alone.
  • When removal makes sense: Removal is recommended for repeated infections of the gum over a part-erupted tooth, decay in the wisdom tooth or the tooth in front that cannot be treated, cysts, or damage to neighbouring teeth.
  • How removal works: Upper wisdom teeth are usually simple to remove under local anaesthetic.

Usually not

Most wisdom teeth that come through without trouble, or that stay buried without symptoms, can be left alone. National guidance in the UK advises against removing healthy wisdom teeth just in case.

When removal makes sense

Removal is recommended for repeated infections of the gum over a part-erupted tooth, decay in the wisdom tooth or the tooth in front that cannot be treated, cysts, or damage to neighbouring teeth.

An X-ray shows the position of the roots and how close they are to the nerve in the lower jaw.

How removal works

Upper wisdom teeth are usually simple to remove under local anaesthetic. Lower teeth that are partly buried may need a small cut in the gum and the tooth divided into pieces. Complex cases are referred to an oral surgeon.

Recovery

Expect swelling and stiffness for three to five days. Use painkillers as advised, eat soft food and start warm salt-water rinses the day after surgery. Avoid smoking for at least two days to lower the risk of a painful dry socket.

When to call us

Contact the clinic if pain gets worse after the third day, if bleeding does not stop with pressure, or if you develop a fever. These can be signs of infection or dry socket, which we can treat quickly.
