Sugar and your teeth

Published 6 May 2025

Key points from this article

  • What sugar does: Bacteria in plaque turn sugar into acid.
  • Frequency matters most: Ten small sugary snacks spread through the day do far more harm than the same amount of sugar eaten at one meal, because the teeth never get time to recover.
  • Hidden sugars: Fruit juices, smoothies, flavoured yoghurts, cereal bars and many sauces contain a lot of sugar.
  • Protecting your teeth: Drink water or milk between meals, chew sugar-free gum after eating to boost saliva, and brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.

What sugar does

Bacteria in plaque turn sugar into acid. Each time you eat or drink something sugary, the acid attacks enamel for up to an hour. Saliva then slowly neutralises the acid and repairs the surface.

Frequency matters most

Ten small sugary snacks spread through the day do far more harm than the same amount of sugar eaten at one meal, because the teeth never get time to recover. Keep sugary foods and drinks to mealtimes where you can.

Hidden sugars

Fruit juices, smoothies, flavoured yoghurts, cereal bars and many sauces contain a lot of sugar. Dried fruit is sticky and clings to teeth. Read labels: anything ending in -ose, plus honey and syrups, is sugar.

Protecting your teeth

Drink water or milk between meals, chew sugar-free gum after eating to boost saliva, and brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.
