First aid for a chipped or cracked tooth

By Dr Rohan Mehta, Implant dentist · Published 26 January 2026 · Updated 4 February 2026

Key points from this article

  • Straight away: Rinse your mouth with warm water.
  • Managing pain: Take paracetamol or ibuprofen as directed on the packet if they are safe for you.
  • How urgent is it: A small chip with no pain can wait for a routine appointment within a week or two.
  • Knocked-out adult tooth: Pick the tooth up by the crown, not the root, rinse briefly in milk and push it back into the socket if you can.
  • What treatment involves: Small chips are repaired with tooth-coloured bonding in one visit.

Straight away

Rinse your mouth with warm water. If there is bleeding, press a clean piece of gauze on the area for ten minutes. Keep any large broken piece in milk and bring it with you; sometimes it can be bonded back.

Managing pain

Take paracetamol or ibuprofen as directed on the packet if they are safe for you. A cold compress on the cheek reduces swelling. Avoid very hot or cold food and chew on the other side.

Sharp edges can be covered with sugar-free chewing gum or dental wax from a pharmacy to protect the tongue and cheek.

How urgent is it

A small chip with no pain can wait for a routine appointment within a week or two. A large break, pain that lasts, or a tooth knocked loose needs to be seen the same or next day.

Knocked-out adult tooth

Pick the tooth up by the crown, not the root, rinse briefly in milk and push it back into the socket if you can. If not, keep it in milk and get to a dentist within an hour.

What treatment involves

Small chips are repaired with tooth-coloured bonding in one visit. Larger breaks may need a crown, and cracks that reach the nerve may need root canal treatment first.
