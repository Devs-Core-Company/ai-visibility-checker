What to expect on implant surgery day

By Dr Rohan Mehta, Implant dentist · Published 22 October 2025 · Updated 15 January 2026

Key points from this article

  • Before the appointment: Eat a normal breakfast and take your usual medicines unless we have told you otherwise.
  • During surgery: After the local anaesthetic has worked, a small opening is made in the gum and a channel prepared in the bone using a surgical guide.
  • The first few days: Expect some swelling and tenderness for three to five days.
  • Follow-up visits: We check healing after ten days and again at eight to twelve weeks, when we take a scan for the final crown.

Before the appointment

Eat a normal breakfast and take your usual medicines unless we have told you otherwise. If you take blood thinners, we will have discussed them with your GP at the planning stage.

Plan to rest for the remainder of the day and arrange for someone to collect you if you are having sedation.

During surgery

After the local anaesthetic has worked, a small opening is made in the gum and a channel prepared in the bone using a surgical guide. The implant is placed and the gum is closed with dissolving stitches. A single implant usually takes about forty-five minutes.

The first few days

Expect some swelling and tenderness for three to five days. Over-the-counter painkillers are usually enough. Use salt-water rinses after meals from the next day and avoid smoking, which greatly increases the risk of the implant failing.

Follow-up visits

We check healing after ten days and again at eight to twelve weeks, when we take a scan for the final crown. Most patients have their permanent tooth about three months after surgery.
