Root canal treatment: myths and facts

By Dr Amelia Hart, Principal dentist · Published 4 August 2026

Key points from this article

  • Is it painful: With modern local anaesthetic, root canal treatment feels much like having a filling.
  • Is extraction better: Keeping your own tooth is almost always better if it can be saved.
  • Does it last: A root-treated back tooth protected with a crown can last for decades.
  • What happens after: The tooth may feel tender for a few days.

Is it painful

With modern local anaesthetic, root canal treatment feels much like having a filling. The infection that leads to it is usually far more painful than the treatment itself.

Is extraction better

Keeping your own tooth is almost always better if it can be saved. Replacing an extracted tooth with an implant or bridge costs more and takes longer.

Does it last

A root-treated back tooth protected with a crown can last for decades. Success rates for first-time treatment are around eighty-five to ninety per cent.

What happens after

The tooth may feel tender for a few days. Back teeth usually need a crown afterwards because they become more brittle once the nerve has been removed.
