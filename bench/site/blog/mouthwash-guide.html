Do you need mouthwash?

By Priya Shah, Dental hygienist · Published 11 March 2025 · Updated 30 March 2025

Key points from this article

  • What mouthwash can do: A fluoride mouthwash adds a little extra protection against decay.
  • What it cannot do: Mouthwash does not remove plaque that is already stuck to teeth and cannot replace brushing and cleaning between the teeth.
  • When to use it: Use mouthwash at a different time from brushing, such as after lunch, so you do not wash away the fluoride from your toothpaste.
  • Alcohol-free or not: Alcohol-free mouthwashes are less likely to sting or dry the mouth.

What mouthwash can do

A fluoride mouthwash adds a little extra protection against decay. Some antibacterial rinses reduce plaque for short periods, for example after gum surgery.

What it cannot do

Mouthwash does not remove plaque that is already stuck to teeth and cannot replace brushing and cleaning between the teeth. Using it to mask bad breath can hide a problem that needs treatment.

When to use it

Use mouthwash at a different time from brushing, such as after lunch, so you do not wash away the fluoride from your toothpaste.

Alcohol-free or not

Alcohol-free mouthwashes are less likely to sting or dry the mouth. Chlorhexidine rinses can stain teeth brown with long-term use and should only be used for short courses when advised by a dentist.
