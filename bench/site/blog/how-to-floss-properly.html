How to floss properly

By Priya Shah, Dental hygienist · Published 14 January 2026 · Updated 2 February 2026

Key points from this article

  • Why cleaning between teeth matters: A toothbrush cleans three of the five surfaces of each tooth.
  • The technique: Wind about 45 centimetres of floss around your middle fingers and hold a short length taut between your thumbs and index fingers.
  • Floss or interdental brushes: Where the gaps between teeth are large enough, small interdental brushes clean better than floss and are easier to use.
  • Bleeding gums: Gums often bleed for the first week or two after you start cleaning between the teeth.

Why cleaning between teeth matters

A toothbrush cleans three of the five surfaces of each tooth. The two surfaces that touch neighbouring teeth are where gum disease and decay most often start, because plaque there is left alone for days at a time.

Cleaning between the teeth once a day, before you brush at night, removes that plaque before it hardens into tartar.

The technique

Wind about 45 centimetres of floss around your middle fingers and hold a short length taut between your thumbs and index fingers. Slide it gently between two teeth, curve it into a C shape against one tooth and move it up and down below the gum line.

Repeat on the neighbouring tooth, then move to a clean section of floss for the next gap. Do not snap the floss down onto the gum, as this causes bleeding and soreness.

Floss or interdental brushes

Where the gaps between teeth are large enough, small interdental brushes clean better than floss and are easier to use. Most adults need two or three different sizes for different parts of the mouth.

Your hygienist can measure the gaps and give you a sample of each size so you can find what fits. If you are not sure which brush to start with, ask at your next visit to our team, or read about our gum treatment service.

Bleeding gums

Gums often bleed for the first week or two after you start cleaning between the teeth. This is a sign of inflammation that usually settles as the gums get healthier. Bleeding that lasts longer than two weeks should be checked by a dentist or hygienist.
