Choosing a toothpaste

By Tom Ellis, Dental therapist · Published 4 May 2026 · Updated 18 May 2026

Key points from this article

  • Fluoride first: The single most important ingredient is fluoride.
  • For sensitive teeth: Toothpastes containing potassium nitrate, stannous fluoride or arginine reduce sensitivity when used twice a day for a few weeks.
  • Whitening and tartar control: Whitening pastes remove surface stain with mild abrasives or enzymes; they do not change the natural shade of the teeth.
  • What you can ignore: Flavour, colour and stripes make no difference to how well a toothpaste works.
  • A simple rule: If it contains the right amount of fluoride and you are happy to use it twice a day, it is a good toothpaste.

Fluoride first

The single most important ingredient is fluoride. Look for 1,350 to 1,500 parts per million on the label for adults and children over seven. Fluoride-free toothpastes do not protect against decay and are not recommended by UK dental guidance.

Children under three need at least 1,000 ppm, used as a smear on the brush.

For sensitive teeth

Toothpastes containing potassium nitrate, stannous fluoride or arginine reduce sensitivity when used twice a day for a few weeks. Keep using them after the sensitivity settles, or it tends to return.

Whitening and tartar control

Whitening pastes remove surface stain with mild abrasives or enzymes; they do not change the natural shade of the teeth. Tartar control pastes slow the build-up of new tartar but cannot remove what is already there.

What you can ignore

Flavour, colour and stripes make no difference to how well a toothpaste works. Natural or herbal labels do not mean safer, and many such products lack fluoride. Price is no guide to effectiveness: supermarket own-brand fluoride pastes work as well as premium ones.

A simple rule

If it contains the right amount of fluoride and you are happy to use it twice a day, it is a good toothpaste. If you have particular problems, such as sensitivity or a high decay rate, ask your dentist or hygienist for a recommendation at your next visit.
