Why are my teeth sensitive?

By Dr Amelia Hart, Principal dentist · Published 9 December 2024 · Updated 3 January 2025

Key points from this article

  • What causes sensitivity: Sensitivity happens when the dentine under the enamel is exposed.
  • Toothpastes that help: Sensitive toothpastes containing potassium nitrate or stannous fluoride work best when used twice a day for several weeks.
  • Treatments in the surgery: Your dentist can paint on a fluoride varnish or seal exposed root surfaces with a thin layer of bonding.
  • When it is something else: Sensitivity in one tooth that lingers after the cold has gone, or that wakes you at night, may be decay or a cracked tooth and should be checked promptly.

What causes sensitivity

Sensitivity happens when the dentine under the enamel is exposed. Tiny channels in the dentine carry temperature changes to the nerve. Gum recession, worn enamel and recent whitening are the usual causes.

Toothpastes that help

Sensitive toothpastes containing potassium nitrate or stannous fluoride work best when used twice a day for several weeks. Rubbing a little on the sensitive area before bed can help too.

Treatments in the surgery

Your dentist can paint on a fluoride varnish or seal exposed root surfaces with a thin layer of bonding. Where gums have receded a lot, a gum graft may be an option.

When it is something else

Sensitivity in one tooth that lingers after the cold has gone, or that wakes you at night, may be decay or a cracked tooth and should be checked promptly.
