Ten common brushing mistakes

By Priya Shah, Dental hygienist · Published 1 July 2025 · Updated 20 July 2025

Key points from this article

  • Technique mistakes: Brushing too hard wears enamel and pushes gums back.
  • Timing mistakes: Brushing straight after acidic food or drink, such as orange juice, can wear softened enamel.
  • Equipment mistakes: A brush with splayed bristles cleans poorly; replace it every three months.
  • Forgetting the rest of the mouth: Plaque collects between the teeth and on the tongue as well as on tooth surfaces.

Technique mistakes

Brushing too hard wears enamel and pushes gums back. Hold the brush like a pen and let the bristles do the work. Brushing side to side across the teeth misses the gum line; use small circles instead and angle the bristles towards the gum.

Rushing is the other common habit. Two minutes feels long, so use a timer or an electric brush with a built-in one.

Timing mistakes

Brushing straight after acidic food or drink, such as orange juice, can wear softened enamel. Wait an hour. Skipping the night-time brush is worse than skipping the morning one, because saliva flow drops during sleep.

Equipment mistakes

A brush with splayed bristles cleans poorly; replace it every three months. Medium or soft bristles are better for most people than hard ones. Sharing a toothbrush spreads bacteria and should be avoided.

Forgetting the rest of the mouth

Plaque collects between the teeth and on the tongue as well as on tooth surfaces. Clean between your teeth daily and brush your tongue gently to reduce bad breath.
