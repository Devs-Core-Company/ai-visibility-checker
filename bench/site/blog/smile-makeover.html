Planning a smile makeover

By Dr Sophie Lang, Orthodontic and cosmetic dentist · Published 1 September 2026 · Updated 5 September 2026

Key points from this article

  • Start with health: Decay and gum disease are treated first.
  • Straighten, whiten, then restore: Moving teeth into better positions with aligners often means less drilling later.
  • Setting goals: We use photographs and a digital simulation to agree what you want to change.
  • Cost and time: A makeover typically takes six to twelve months.

Start with health

Decay and gum disease are treated first. Cosmetic work on unhealthy teeth does not last, and gum inflammation makes it hard to match colours and shapes.

Straighten, whiten, then restore

Moving teeth into better positions with aligners often means less drilling later. Whitening comes next, because bonding and veneers are matched to the final shade.

Setting goals

We use photographs and a digital simulation to agree what you want to change. A trial smile in the mouth lets you see proposed changes before any irreversible work.

Cost and time

A makeover typically takes six to twelve months. We give a written plan with each stage priced separately so you can spread treatment over time.

Before treatment After treatment

Before: crowded lower teeth, worn edges on the upper front teeth and yellow staining.

After: aligners for eight months, home whitening for two weeks and composite bonding on four upper teeth.
