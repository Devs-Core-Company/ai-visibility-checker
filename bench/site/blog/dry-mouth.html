Living with a dry mouth

By Dr Amelia Hart, Principal dentist · Published 21 January 2025 · Updated 10 February 2025

Key points from this article

  • Why saliva matters: Saliva washes away food, neutralises acid and helps repair enamel.
  • Common causes: More than four hundred common medicines list dry mouth as a side effect, including some antidepressants, blood pressure tablets and antihistamines.
  • What helps: Sip water often, chew sugar-free gum, and avoid alcohol-based mouthwashes and caffeine late in the day.
  • Protecting your teeth: Your dentist may prescribe a high-fluoride toothpaste and suggest more frequent check-ups.

Why saliva matters

Saliva washes away food, neutralises acid and helps repair enamel. Without enough of it, decay can develop quickly, especially around the gum line and on root surfaces.

Common causes

More than four hundred common medicines list dry mouth as a side effect, including some antidepressants, blood pressure tablets and antihistamines. Breathing through the mouth, radiotherapy to the head and neck and some medical conditions also reduce saliva.

What helps

Sip water often, chew sugar-free gum, and avoid alcohol-based mouthwashes and caffeine late in the day. Saliva substitute gels and sprays can make the mouth more comfortable at night.

Protecting your teeth

Your dentist may prescribe a high-fluoride toothpaste and suggest more frequent check-ups. Ask your GP whether any of your medicines could be changed.
