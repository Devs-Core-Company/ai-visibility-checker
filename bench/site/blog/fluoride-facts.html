Fluoride facts

By Dr Amelia Hart, Principal dentist · Published 12 August 2025 · Updated 4 October 2025

Key points from this article

  • How fluoride works: Fluoride helps repair the early stages of tooth decay by making enamel harder and more resistant to acid.
  • The right strength: Adults and children over seven should use toothpaste containing 1,350 to 1,500 parts per million fluoride.

How fluoride works

Fluoride helps repair the early stages of tooth decay by making enamel harder and more resistant to acid. It works mostly at the tooth surface, which is why fluoride toothpaste is so effective.

The right strength

Adults and children over seven should use toothpaste containing 1,350 to 1,500 parts per million fluoride. Younger children should use at least 1,000 ppm. People at high risk of decay may be prescribed 2,800 or 5,000 ppm toothpaste.

Spit, don’t rinse

After brushing, spit out the excess toothpaste but do not rinse with water. Rinsing washes away the fluoride left on the teeth. If you use a mouthwash, use it at a different time of day.

Is fluoride safe

At the levels in toothpaste and in fluoridated water supplies, fluoride is safe and well studied. Young children should be supervised so they use the right amount and do not swallow it.
