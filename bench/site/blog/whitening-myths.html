Teeth whitening myths, checked

By Dr Sophie Lang, Orthodontic and cosmetic dentist · Published 18 September 2025 · Updated 1 December 2025

Key points from this article

  • Myth one: whitening damages enamel: Peroxide gels used under dental supervision do not soften or thin enamel when used as directed.
  • Myth two: charcoal toothpaste whitens: Charcoal pastes remove some surface stain by abrasion, but they do not change the colour of the tooth itself.
  • Myth three: results are permanent: Teeth slowly pick up stain again after whitening.
  • Myth four: anyone can whiten: Whitening is not suitable during pregnancy, for under-eighteens except to treat disease, or where there is untreated decay or gum disease.
  • Myth five: beauty salons are a safe alternative: In the UK it is illegal for anyone other than a dental professional to provide whitening.

Myth one: whitening damages enamel

Peroxide gels used under dental supervision do not soften or thin enamel when used as directed. Short-lived sensitivity is common but is caused by the gel reaching the nerve through the tooth, not by damage to the surface.

Myth two: charcoal toothpaste whitens

Charcoal pastes remove some surface stain by abrasion, but they do not change the colour of the tooth itself. Many contain no fluoride, and frequent use can wear the enamel, making teeth look more yellow over time.

Myth three: results are permanent

Teeth slowly pick up stain again after whitening. Most people top up with a night or two of tray wear every six to twelve months, especially if they drink coffee, tea or red wine.

Myth four: anyone can whiten

Whitening is not suitable during pregnancy, for under-eighteens except to treat disease, or where there is untreated decay or gum disease. It also does not change crowns or fillings, which may need replacing to match.

Myth five: beauty salons are a safe alternative

In the UK it is illegal for anyone other than a dental professional to provide whitening. Salon treatments either use ineffective products or break the law, and they cannot check whether your teeth are healthy enough to whiten.
