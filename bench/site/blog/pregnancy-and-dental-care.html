Looking after your teeth in pregnancy

By Priya Shah, Dental hygienist · Published 16 March 2026 · Updated 28 March 2026

Key points from this article

Pregnancy gingivitis

Hormone changes make the gums react more strongly to plaque, so many women notice bleeding gums from the second month. Careful brushing and cleaning between the teeth keeps it under control, and a hygiene visit helps.

Morning sickness

Stomach acid softens enamel. After being sick, rinse with water or a fluoride mouthwash rather than brushing straight away, and wait an hour before brushing.

Is treatment safe

Check-ups, cleaning and fillings are safe during pregnancy, and the second trimester is usually the most comfortable time for longer treatment. We postpone routine X-rays unless they are needed to diagnose a problem.

Tell us you are pregnant so we can choose suitable local anaesthetics and medicines.

Cravings and snacks

Frequent snacking on sugary foods raises the risk of decay. Choose savoury snacks where you can and drink water or milk between meals.

After the birth

Book a check-up when you can after your baby arrives. Bring your baby along to your own appointments so they get used to the surroundings before their first visit.
