Dental care for children

We like to see children from the time their first tooth appears. Early visits are short and relaxed, and they help children get used to the sights and sounds of a dental surgery.

Preventing decay

Fluoride varnish painted on the teeth twice a year strengthens enamel. Fissure sealants fill the deep grooves on new back teeth so food and bacteria cannot settle there.

Tips for parents

Brush twice a day with a smear of fluoride toothpaste under three and a pea-sized amount from three to six. Keep sugary food and drinks to mealtimes and avoid drinks other than milk or water at bedtime.

Children cannot brush well on their own until about seven years old, so help with the evening brush until then.

Who provides children's dentistry

Tom Ellis and our dental therapists leads this service, which is available at all three clinics. Every clinician who treats you is registered with the General Dental Council, and you can ask to see the same person at each visit.

Your first appointment

We start with a conversation about what you would like to change and any worries you have, followed by an examination. You will leave with a written plan that sets out the options, how long each takes and the fee for each stage. There is no obligation to go ahead, and you can take the plan away to think about it.

Questions worth asking

Ask what the alternatives are, including doing nothing, how long the result is expected to last, and what the follow-up visits involve. Our team would much rather answer questions before treatment than after it.

Fees and booking

The current fee for children's dentistry is listed on our prices page, and your dentist will confirm it in writing before treatment. You can book online or call any of our clinics.
